# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository contains the project management playbooks and process guidance used by OctoAcme. The goal of these documents is to help the team align on how work is initiated, planned, executed, released, and improved over time.

## Project Management Process Summary

OctoAcme uses a lightweight, cross-functional project lifecycle that begins with initiation and moves through planning, execution, release, and retrospective improvement. In the initiation phase, the team validates the business need, identifies stakeholders, defines success metrics, and decides whether to move forward with deeper planning. Once approved, the team creates a backlog, identifies dependencies, estimates work, and outlines milestones and acceptance criteria so delivery can stay focused and measurable.

During execution, OctoAcme emphasizes clear ownership, regular communication, and quality gates. The team works in a rhythm of standups, syncs, reviews, and risk tracking, with project managers coordinating delivery and product owners prioritizing value. Core roles include Product Managers, Project Managers, Developers, QA/testing, and stakeholders, each contributing to a shared goal of predictable, customer-focused delivery. Communication is structured through recurring updates, issue tracking, escalations for blockers, and a single source of truth for project status.

Quality assurance is built into the workflow, not treated as a final step. Teams are expected to define clear acceptance criteria, use CI to check tests and security scanning, require code review and approval before merge, and run smoke tests for critical workflows before a release. Risk management and stakeholder communication are handled through a formal risk register, escalation paths, and regular status reporting so cross-team dependencies and business impact are visible early.

At release time, OctoAcme validates readiness with deployment checks, smoke tests, release notes, rollback planning, and post-deploy verification. After each sprint, milestone, or incident, the team conducts a retrospective to capture lessons learned and convert them into action items that improve delivery. This creates a continuous improvement loop that keeps the process practical, repeatable, and aligned with customer and business outcomes.
